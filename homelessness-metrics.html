📊 Data Methodology

This analysis presents funding data by fiscal year (July 1 - June 30), compiled from official government sources including HUD awards, Metro Nashville budgets, and council resolutions. Figures were reviewed by former senior employees of Metro Nashville's homeless approach and were deemed substantially accurate, though particulars of calendar years and fiscal and actual spend down may vary. The arc of funding dramatically increasing with housing placements remaining static is accurate.

Understanding the Data

Fiscal Year Timeline

Data follows Metro Nashville's fiscal year (July 1 - June 30). For example, FY2024 runs from July 1, 2023 to June 30, 2024.

ESG-CARES COVID Relief

$10 million total in two allocations (summer 2020), deployed over two-year grant period FY2021-2022. "Housing Surge" began October 1, 2020.

ARPA Timeline & Spending

$50M authorized October 2022 (FY2023). Through November 2023, only $8.2M was spent due to contracting delays. Bulk of spending occurred in FY2024. The $25M PSH gap financing created only 182 units over 3+ years at $132,798 per unit.

HUD CoC Awards

Federal awards flow directly to nonprofit agencies and MDHA. FY2024 shows combined FY23 ($9.7M) and FY24 ($11.8M) awards.

Metro Budget Evolution

Metro General Fund support grew from $2.4M (FY2020) to $5.5M (FY2024). Office of Homeless Services launched FY2024.

Strobel House

90 studio units serving 6.1% of chronically homeless (90 of 1,472 as of April 2025). $500K operations startup funding included in FY2024. Opened July 18, 2024. $35M capital construction costs tracked separately from operational funding.

Key Funding Programs

HUD Continuum of Care (CoC)

Annual federal competitive grants for permanent supportive housing, rapid rehousing, and support services. Direct to providers.

ESG-CARES COVID Relief

Emergency federal funding totaling $10M in two rounds for pandemic homelessness response. Two-year grant period FY2021-2022.

ARPA Homelessness Initiative

$50M allocation: PSH Gap Financing ($25M - created only 182 units at $132,798/unit), Interim Housing ($9M), Housing First Services ($9M), Low-Barrier Grants ($7M). Gap financing structured as loans, not grants.

Mobile Navigation Centers

$850K funding (FY2022) for mobile outreach centers announced as part of Mayor's homelessness response plan to connect unsheltered individuals with services.

Unsheltered Supplement

Special $4.9M HUD grant (FY2023) specifically targeting unsheltered homelessness through special NOFO, supplementing regular CoC awards.

Barnes Fund

Nashville's affordable housing trust fund, Round 14 allocated $7.6M for permanent supportive housing projects.

⚠️ Important Notes

  • Despite 428% funding increase, homelessness increased 3.8% (2,017 → 2,094)
  • Strobel House: $35M construction investment houses 90 people (6.1% of chronically homeless)
  • ARPA PSH Gap Financing: $25M created only 182 units over 3+ years at $132,798 per unit PLUS requires $15,358/year ongoing
  • Chronic homelessness: 1,472 individuals as of April 2025
  • HUD CoC funds flow directly to nonprofits, not through Metro budget
  • ESG-CARES: $10M total split over FY2021-2022
  • ARPA actual spending lagged authorization by over a year
  • Cost per person varies dramatically: $9,058 (FY2020) to $24,491 (FY2024)
  • Some Metro General Fund amounts could not be verified from budget documents

📈 Key Insights from Reviewed Data

  • Funding increased 428% from FY2020 to FY2024 ($8.8M → $46.6M)
  • People housed increased only 95% in the same period (975 → 1,902)
  • Homelessness actually increased: Up 3.8% (2,017 → 2,094)
  • FY2021 Housing Surge impact: ESG-CARES enabled housing 1,875 people
  • Cost per person nearly tripled from FY2020 to FY2024 ($9,058 → $24,491)
  • FY2024 vs National Benchmark: At RAND study rate of $15,358/person, the $46.6M could have housed 3,032 people instead of 1,902
  • Lost capacity: 1,130 additional people could have been housed at best practice rates
  • Chronic homelessness crisis: 1,472 chronically homeless (April 2025)
  • Strobel House impact: $35M facility houses 90 people (6.1% of chronically homeless)
  • ARPA PSH double burden: $25M gap financing created only 182 units at $132,798/unit PLUS requires $15,358/year ongoing operations
  • Total housed 9,509 people over 5 years at average cost of $12,784

Complete Documentation & Sources

ARPA PSH Gap Financing Documentation
  • Authorizing Resolution: RS2022-1696 - $25M for PSH/0-30% AMI Housing
  • Implementation Report: OHS American Rescue Plan Report (May 2025)
  • Program Structure: Low-interest loans to developers (not grants), with up to $5M forgivable
  • MDHA Administration: 5% fee ($1.25M) for program administration
  • Actual Deployment: $24,169,120 allocated across 10 projects creating 182 units (124 PSH + 58 deeply affordable)
  • Timeline: Units coming online May 2023 - December 2025 (3+ years from authorization)
  • Per Unit Cost: $132,798 as gap financing (total development costs higher with other funding sources) PLUS $15,358/year ongoing operations
  • True 10-Year Cost: $286,378 per unit ($132,798 capital + 10 years × $15,358 operations)
  • Status: Many units marked "Now Online" per OHS report, but full deployment extends to Dec 2025
National Benchmark Sources
  • RAND Corporation Study (2020): Cost after PSH placement: $15,358/person/year
  • Study Details: "Cost Analysis of Health Care Utilization Among Individuals Experiencing Chronic Homelessness" - Western Journal of Emergency Medicine
  • Methodology: Analysis of 107 chronically homeless adults in supportive housing, tracking costs 12 months before and after placement
  • Key Finding: 60% reduction in public costs after housing placement (from $38,146 to $15,358 annually)
