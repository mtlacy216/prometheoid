📊 Data Methodology

This dashboard presents verified funding data by fiscal year (July 1 - June 30), compiled from official government sources including HUD awards, Metro Nashville budgets, and council resolutions. All figures are documented with source URLs in the Sources tab.

Understanding the Data

Fiscal Year Timeline

Data follows Metro Nashville's fiscal year (July 1 - June 30). For example, FY2024 runs from July 1, 2023 to June 30, 2024.

FY2021 COVID Relief Impact

The $7.7 million in ESG-CARES and other COVID funding enabled housing 1,875 people at $9,751 per person - near peak efficiency.

ARPA Timeline & Spending

$50M authorized October 2022 (FY2023). Through December 31, 2023, $18.3M was spent. Through November 2024, $29.4M has been spent/committed with remaining funds through 2026.

HUD CoC Awards

Federal awards flow directly to nonprofit agencies and MDHA. FY2024 shows combined FY23 ($9.7M) and FY24 ($11.8M) awards.

Metro Budget Evolution

Metro General Fund support grew from $2.4M (FY2020) to $5.5M (FY2024). Office of Homeless Services launched FY2024.

Strobel House

90 studio units serving 6.1% of chronically homeless (90 of 1,472 as of April 2025). $500K operations startup funding included in FY2024. Opened July 18, 2024. $35M capital construction costs tracked separately from operational funding.

Key Funding Programs

HUD Continuum of Care (CoC)

Annual federal competitive grants for permanent supportive housing, rapid rehousing, and support services. Direct to providers.

ESG-CARES COVID Relief

Emergency federal funding totaling $10M in two rounds ($1.5M + $8.5M) for pandemic homelessness response.

ARPA Homelessness Initiative

$50M allocation: PSH Gap Financing ($25M), Interim Housing ($9M), Housing First Services ($9M), Low-Barrier Grants ($7M).

Mobile Navigation Centers

$850K funding (FY2022) for mobile outreach centers announced as part of Mayor's homelessness response plan to connect unsheltered individuals with services.

Unsheltered Supplement

Special $4.9M HUD grant (FY2023) specifically targeting unsheltered homelessness through special NOFO, supplementing regular CoC awards.

Barnes Fund

Nashville's affordable housing trust fund, Round 14 allocated $7.6M for permanent supportive housing projects.

MHID Special Purpose Fund

External grants including HUD HMIS ($141K) and Coordinated Entry ($128K) for system infrastructure.

⚠️ Important Notes

  • Despite 428% funding increase, homelessness increased 3.8% (2,017 → 2,094)
  • Strobel House: $35M capital investment serves only 6.1% of chronically homeless
  • Chronic homelessness: 1,472 individuals as of April 2025 (48% experiencing chronic homelessness)
  • HUD CoC funds flow directly to nonprofits, not through Metro budget
  • COVID funding split between ESG-CARES ($10M) and other COVID programs ($13.2M)
  • ARPA actual spending: $8.2M in FY2023, $21.2M in FY2024 (of $50M authorized)
  • Cost per person varies dramatically: $8,951 (FY2021) to $24,491 (FY2024)

📈 Key Insights from Verified Data

  • Funding increased 428% from FY2020 to FY2024 ($8.8M → $46.6M)
  • People housed increased only 95% in the same period (975 → 1,902)
  • Homelessness actually increased: Up 3.8% (2,017 → 2,094)
  • FY2021 was most efficient: $8,951 per person housed
  • Cost per person nearly tripled from FY2021 to FY2024 ($8,951 → $24,491)
  • Chronic homelessness crisis: 1,472 chronically homeless (April 2025)
  • Strobel House impact: $35M facility serves only 6.1% of chronically homeless
  • Total housed 9,509 people over 5 years at average cost of $13,349
